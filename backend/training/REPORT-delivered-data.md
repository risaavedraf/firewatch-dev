`nro_inc_act` is a season-cumulative counter, so the label is its day-to-day advance
clamped at zero. `sup_afec_act` is cumulative too and was revised **downward** on the
rows counted above; each such difference is clamped to 0 and **counted**, because a
silently dropped negative difference is burned hectares that left the record without
a trace. The clamp count is the number in that row: it is reported, never absorbed.

The other thing this file invites is leakage, and the guard that blocks it is measured rather than assumed. On the committed fixture, adding `nro_inc_act` to the legal features lifts September PR-AUC from 0.0826 to about 0.31, and adding its daily difference -- which is the label by construction -- reproduces the label exactly. `tests/test_train_delivered.py` pins both bounds, so the forbidden-column guard protects a number somebody can see.

## The split

| Side | Months | Region-days | Positives | Base rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Train | July, August | 992 | 27 | 2.72% |
| Test | September | 448 | 42 | 9.38% |

Temporal, never random: September follows August, and a random split would let the
model learn a September day from a July one. The run asserts the split is disjoint
and strictly ordered before it fits anything.

The base rate is 3.4x higher in the month the model is judged on (9.38% against 2.72%), because the season ramps up. A threshold chosen on July and August therefore under-alerts in September, which is exactly what a recall of 0.0000 looks like when it happens.

### Beats, pair by pair

| Model | Against | Beats it? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| logistic | climatology | no | Brier 0.1754 does not undercut 0.0952 |
| logistic | region_frequency | no | Brier 0.1754 does not undercut 0.0882 |
| forest | climatology | no | PR-AUC 0.1246 does not exceed 0.1766 |
| forest | region_frequency | no | PR-AUC 0.1246 does not exceed 0.1261; Brier 0.0928 does not undercut 0.0882 |

Beating a baseline requires **both** a higher PR-AUC and a lower Brier. A higher
PR-AUC at a worse Brier is a trade, and calling it a win is how a model gets
registered for the wrong reason.

Verdict: neither trained model beats either baseline on this data. logistic vs climatology: Brier 0.1754 does not undercut 0.0952. logistic vs region_frequency: Brier 0.1754 does not undercut 0.0882. forest vs climatology: PR-AUC 0.1246 does not exceed 0.1766. forest vs region_frequency: PR-AUC 0.1246 does not exceed 0.1261; Brier 0.0928 does not undercut 0.0882. The closest is logistic against region_frequency: it leads PR-AUC by 0.0540 and trails Brier by 0.0872. A lead on one axis with a loss on the other is a trade, not a win. No model here improves on climatology, and that is the finding, not a failure of the pipeline.

## The exported artifact

`delivered-data-rehearsal-v1` (`logistic`), sha256 `33970a895cd4ea280e5b116bdf4477204f42a38d40efe06c89d8a1bba65575e2`.

It is a **rehearsal** and it is **not servable by `risk`**: its feature set includes a
one-hot of region, and `risk` builds its input vector from a publication with no region
identity. It must never be registered in `models` and never activated. It exists so the
exporter is proven against the Go artifact reader, which is exactly what the tests do:
the emitted document is re-read with a Python transcription of `artifact.Validate`
(`backend/training/model_contract.py`) and refused if a single rule fails.

The marker lives in `version` (`rehearsal` in the string) and in `metrics.rehearsal`,
`metrics.servable_by_risk`, `metrics.not_servable_reason` and
`metrics.must_not_be_registered` -- **in the artifact's own bytes**, not only here. A
top-level `rehearsal` key is not possible: the Go reader decodes with
`DisallowUnknownFields`, so an undefined key makes the document unreadable, and an
unreadable artifact is worse than a marked one.

`center`/`scale`/`impute` were fitted on the **training split only**, in that order of
documented preprocessing (`NaN -> impute`, then `(x - center) / scale`). The declared
`min`/`max` guards are train-only too. 16 rows have no
climatology rate (the window's first day has no predecessor); they are all in the
training split, and the run refuses to score a test row that has none.

One note for whoever exports a forest next: scikit-learn walks a tree in `float32` while the Go scorer walks `float64`, so a forest artifact can take the other branch on a row whose value sits within one float32 step of a threshold. Measured on the committed fixture, that moved one row's probability by 0.30. This run exported a `logistic` model, which has no thresholds at all and therefore no such divergence.

## What is missing

Each gap is named with what it would enable, because a list of absences without a
consequence is a complaint rather than a plan.

**Region identity at inference time.** The one-hot of region is the reason this artifact cannot be served: `risk` builds its vector from a publication and carries no region, so every region indicator would arrive empty. What it would enable: a model that can be served at all.

**A comuna-level key, and the geometry or fuel data behind it.** Region is a coarse proxy for 16 very different fire regimes; Atacama and Magallanes never advanced the counter in these 90 days while Araucania did 13 times. What it would enable: a model that generalises between places instead of memorising that Araucania is the one that burns.

**Meteorology on days without a fire.** No column of this export carries weather, and the CONAF event registry only carries weather attached to an event. What it would enable: the seven features the artifact contract already declares, and therefore an input that is not a calendar.

**Accumulated precipitation, 7 days, and a daily extreme.** The dominant driver of fire activity is not in the file in any form. What it would enable: the one feature family that separates a dry September from a wet one, which is exactly the variation a 90-day window cannot learn.

**More than one season, and more than 90 days.** One ramp-up from July to September cannot separate a seasonal effect from a year. Both models here can only learn how far into the season the date is. What it would enable: inter-annual variation, and a test split that is a season rather than a month.

**The dated, access-gated CONAF registry (DOI 10.71578/UXAUN5).** The label here is a counter's daily difference in one region, not an event with a cause, a location and an area. What it would enable: a real comuna-day label across 19 seasons, and the per-event fields the panel assembler already knows how to read.

**Human-ignition, population, road-access and suppression data.** Most Chilean fires are human-caused, and the counter cannot tell a lightning strike from an escaped burn. What it would enable: a feature set that is causal rather than correlational, and an honest answer to why one region burns and another does not.

**Forecast weather instead of observed weather.** Even a perfect history answers what happened, and the product's question is tomorrow. What it would enable: a lead time. Nothing in this file is forward-looking.
